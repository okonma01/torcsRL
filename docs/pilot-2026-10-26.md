# First driving trial — 26 October 2026

We ran a small trial before committing to the full reward-comparison project.
The goal was to check that the setup works and measure how much time it needs.

## What we did

- Used one track, Alpine-1, with the existing reward and driving rules.
- Checked the car once before training and once afterward, using the same test settings.
- Collected 1,000 initial practice steps, followed by 10,000 steps with learning.
- Used the Mac's graphics processor for learning and the local Linux simulator for driving.
- Kept this run separate from earlier saved runs.

## What happened

The setup check passed: 100 driving steps and a learning update completed successfully.
The training portion, including initial practice and restarts, took 344 seconds
(about 5 minutes 44 seconds). It collected about 32 driving steps per second.
That is computer running time, not time spent driving inside the game.

| Measurement | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| Distance in the check before training | 0.564 metres |
| Distance in the check after training | 0.087 metres |
| Completed practice attempts during training | 87 |
| Completed laps during training or the two checks | 0 |

Some practice attempts travelled farther, but they included random movements.
The final check did not show better driving. The car barely moved in either
check; neither reported leaving the road or taking new damage.
No simulator communication failures were reported during the run.

This was one short run with one starting seed and one check at each end. It
does not establish that the approach cannot learn, or how much training a lap
will require. We have not compared rewards yet.

## What this tells us

The setup can run a measured training trial and save its results. This short
trial did not teach the car to drive reliably. The next useful investigation is
why it struggles to get moving and keep moving, before scheduling long runs or
making lap-time promises to the professor. Keep the current reward as the
starting point while investigating that issue.

## Saved evidence

Run folder: `checkpoints/pilots/20261026T184633768765Z/`.

- `settings.json`: the settings and main software versions.
- `attempts.jsonl`: every completed practice or checking attempt; an unfinished
  practice attempt at the end of the training budget is not included.
- `summary.json`: before/after results and running time.
- `source.zip` and `source-sha256.json`: a copy of the relevant program files
  and their fingerprints.
- `checkpoints/nsteptd3/`: the saved driving model and learning records.

The saved models were loaded successfully and checked for invalid numbers.
The saved attempt records agree with the summary. Failed laps have no recorded
lap time, so a failure cannot appear to be a fast lap.

To repeat the trial from the repository directory while the simulator is running:

```sh
.venv/bin/python -u -m scripts.pilot --steps 10000
```

Each invocation creates a new results folder. The pilot runner adds recording
and before/after checks; it does not change the existing reward or learning method.
